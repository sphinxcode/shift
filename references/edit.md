# EDIT: rewrite for standing

Run `references/clear.md` first.

For every sentence, ask one question: after reading this, is the reader larger,
level, or smaller? Most bad copy is level. Level is the failure state, not the
safe state.

---

## Content patterns

### 1. Trait praise where act praise belongs

**Watch:** *you have a gift for, you're naturally, you're the kind of person
who, your innate, it's just who you are, you were born to, gifted, talented,
special.*

**Problem:** Fixed attributions build the fragile kind of pride. A reader cannot
repeat a trait. They can repeat an act.

> **Before:** You're a natural leader.
> **After:** You said the thing nobody else would say in that meeting.

### 2. Contingent regard

**Watch:** *when you finally, once you've, if you're serious about, ready to
commit, those who truly, the ones who show up.*

**Problem:** Acceptance offered on condition is evaluation wearing warmth. The
reader hears the condition first, and hears that they currently fall short.

> **Before:** For those ready to do the real work.
> **After:** Wherever you are with it today is a fine place to start.

### 3. Diagnosis without return

**Watch:** *your blocks, what's holding you back, the pattern you keep
repeating, your shadow, your wound, self-sabotage.*

**Problem:** Naming a difficulty and stopping leaves the reader opened and
abandoned. Any hard observation completes into what it also gives them. An
unfinished arc is the most diminishing thing writing can do.

> **Before:** You take on everyone else's stress. That's exhausting.
> **After:** You take on everyone else's stress, which wears you out. It also
> means you read a room faster than almost anyone in it.

### 4. Concepts wearing costumes

**Watch:** any sentence opening *you know that feeling when* or *you know that
pull to,* then naming an abstraction.

**Problem:** A scene shaped sentence containing a definition. It has the grammar
of recognition with none of the content. The reader nods without being seen.

> **Before:** You know that pull to step into group dynamics and sense where
> things need directing.
> **After:** You're the one who notices someone went quiet at dinner, and checks
> in later, privately, not at the table.

**The test:** could a stranger have written this about anyone? Then it is a
concept in costume. Aim for concrete enough to be wrong.

### 5. Counting where naming belongs

**Watch:** a bare quantity offered as a feature. *Five different things, nine
sections, forty-one pages, over 200 templates, 10,000 codes.*

**Problem:** A count hands the reader a magnitude to measure themselves against,
and flattens distinct things into units. Enumeration also lets the writer avoid
having a view. List five and one might land.

> **Before:** Chart five different relationship types.
> **After:** Chart the people you're trying to understand. A partner, a client,
> your whole team.

### 6. Promised feeling

**Watch:** *feel confident, feel calm, feel empowered, experience clarity,
unlock your potential, transform your, radiant, abundant, aligned.*

**Problem:** Asserting an emotional outcome puts the reader in debt. If they do
not feel it, they have failed your copy. You cannot install a state by naming
it. Build the conditions and let it arrive.

> **Before:** Feel confident and clear every morning.
> **After:** Two minutes, before anything else asks for you.

## Position patterns

### 7. System as subject

**Watch:** any sentence whose grammatical subject is a feature, screen, button
or product. *Enter sends. The switcher opens. New chart creates. This panel
shows.*

**Problem:** The reader becomes a spectator to their own tool. Google, Apple and
Microsoft all put the reader in subject position in their documentation styles
for this reason.

> **Before:** Enter sends, Shift and Enter makes a line.
> **After:** Send with Enter. Hold Shift if you're still writing.

### 8. Controlling verbs

**Watch:** *must, should, need to, have to, make sure you, don't forget to, be
sure to, remember to.*

**Problem:** Pressuring language reduces the reader's sense of agency.
Autonomy supportive phrasing gives a reason, acknowledges their view, and leaves
the choice with them. *You might, one option is, if it helps, it's yours to
decide.*

> **Before:** You should complete your profile to get accurate results.
> **After:** Birth time sharpens the reading. You can add it whenever you like.

### 9. Presupposed deficiency

**Watch:** *simply, just, easily, obviously, of course, all you have to do,
don't worry.*

**Problem:** These smuggle in an assumption about the reader's position.
*Simply* implies anyone competent finds this easy, so struggling means you are
not. *Don't worry* installs the worry.

> **Before:** Just tap the icon, it's easy.
> **After:** Tap the icon when you want it.

### 10. Copulas and transactional verbs

**Watch:** *is, means, makes, costs, provides, enables, allows, delivers,
offers, features.*

**Problem:** They assert a static relation between two nouns and depict no
event. Nothing happens, so nothing lands.

> **Before:** A corner mark means Pro.
> **After:** Open anything with a corner mark and look around. Pro brings the
> reading with it.

### 11. Missing stance

**Problem:** Machine prose measurably under-uses hedges, boosters and attitude
markers, and those markers are where affect lives in a sentence. Strip them and
you get text that is correct, complete and dead. This is the mechanism behind
"robotic" far more than vocabulary is.

> **Before:** The counter shows your position in the section.
> **After:** Sections run deeper than they look. The counter keeps your place.

A sentence issued from nowhere by no one reads as issued by no one.

### 12. Premature closure

**Problem:** Machine prose resolves to tidy completion in roughly three quarters
of cases whether or not the material warrants it. A sentence that closes
completely invites nothing, and leaves no room for the reader's actual
situation.

> **Before:** Shift and Enter makes a line.
> **After:** Hold Shift if you're still mid-thought.

## Commercial register

The line is not vocabulary. Advertising is often skilful, playful, genuinely
literary writing. What marks it is an appeal to the addressee with a clock
attached. Sincere registers, liturgy and poetry and a letter between friends,
use the same rhythm and repetition and decline the imperative and the deadline.

### 13. Urgency and scarcity

**Watch:** *limited time, only N left, offer ends, act now, don't miss, last
chance, for the next 30 minutes, while spots last.*

> **Before:** 70% off, for the next 30 minutes.
> **After:** The annual plan is 70% less. It'll be here when you want it.

### 14. Benefit stacking

**Watch:** rapid outcome lists, tick ladders, *plus, and, unlimited, exclusive
access, everything you need.*

**Problem:** Volume standing in for a claim worth making. Readers skim a stack
and retain none of it.

> **Before:** Daily mantras, personal journal, unlimited rituals, real-time
> intention setting.
> **After:** One word each morning, drawn from your own chart. Somewhere to
> write down what it moved.

### 15. Superlative and comparison

**Watch:** *best, most powerful, revolutionary, groundbreaking, unlike anything,
the only app that, world-class, cutting-edge.*

**Problem:** Comparative claims put the reader in a shopping posture. Sincere
registers describe instead of ranking.

> **Before:** The most advanced Human Design platform available.
> **After:** Your chart, and someone to ask about it.

### 16. Borrowed authority

**Watch:** *science confirms, studies show, research proves, backed by science,
clinically proven,* with no study named.

**Problem:** Invoking science without a citation is a costume, and it breaks
badly. A reader who checks and finds nothing discounts everything else you said.

> **Before:** Science confirms: when you begin within, everything shifts.
> **After:** Fifteen minutes of writing about what you value has shown effects
> on wellbeing years later. This is a smaller version of that.

## Process

1. Read the whole piece for standing. Note where the reader is left smaller.
2. If a voice sample exists, read it first. Match its sentence lengths,
   punctuation habits, vocabulary level. Shifting register never means
   overwriting someone's voice with yours.
3. Pass against patterns 1 to 16.
4. Draft.
5. **Audit one.** Ask yourself: *"Where does this still leave the reader smaller,
   or level?"* Answer in bullets.
6. **Audit two.** Ask: *"Which of these sentences could a stranger have written
   about anyone?"* Answer in bullets.
7. Revise against both.
8. Run the counts.
9. Report.

## The counts

- trait attributions: **zero**
- conditions on acceptance: **zero**
- urgency or scarcity markers: **zero**
- unnamed appeals to science: **zero**
- sentences whose subject is a UI element: **zero**
- hard observations left without their return: **zero**
- at least one sentence inviting the reader's own recall: **one or more**

## Output

Produce all four.

1. **Draft**
2. **Audit**, both passes, as bullets
3. **Final**
4. **Changes**, a table of each change and which position it serves

## Worked example

**Input**

> Unlock your potential with our revolutionary personalized manifestation
> system! You have a natural gift for intuition, you just haven't tapped into it
> yet. Science confirms that when you begin within, everything shifts. Simply
> complete your profile and our AI will generate your personal codes. Daily
> mantras, unlimited rituals, personal journal. 70% OFF, next 30 minutes only.

**Draft**

> Your chart holds words that are specific to you. Complete your birth details
> and we'll find them. Each morning there's one word, a short practice, and
> somewhere to write down what it moved. The annual plan is 70% less.

**Audit one, where is the reader still smaller or level?**

- "we'll find them" puts us in the subject position and the reader in the
  passenger seat. They should be the one finding.
- The middle is a feature list in prose clothing. Level, not lifting.
- Nothing invites their own recall. All assertion.
- "Complete your birth details" is a bare imperative with no rationale.

**Audit two, what could a stranger have written about anyone?**

- All of it. Not one line only this reader could receive.

**Final**

> Think of the last time you knew something before you could explain how.
>
> That's the part of you this reads from. Give us your birth date, time and
> place, and each morning you'll get one word drawn from it, with somewhere to
> write down what it moved.
>
> Birth time sharpens it. You can add that later if you have to go looking.
>
> The annual plan is 70% less, and it'll be there when you want it.

**Changes**

| Change | Position served |
|---|---|
| Opened with a question sending them to their own memory | Sovereignty. The evidence is theirs, not our claim |
| Cut "you have a natural gift" | Sovereignty. Trait praise builds the fragile kind |
| Cut "you just haven't tapped into it yet" | Regard. That is a condition, and a verdict |
| Cut the unnamed "science confirms" | Regard. Borrowed authority is a costume |
| Cut "simply" | Regard. Presupposed the reader's ease |
| Birth time offered with a reason, no pressure | Sovereignty. Autonomy supportive |
| Deadline dropped, price kept | Regard. The fact survives, the clock does not |
| Checklist became one specific morning | Expanse. One real thing opens wider than four abstract ones |
